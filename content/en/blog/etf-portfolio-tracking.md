# ETF Portfolio Tracking for Self-Directed Investors

For self-directed investors, Exchange-Traded Funds (ETFs) offer a powerful way to build diversified, low-cost portfolios. However, as your portfolio grows, keeping track of asset allocation, performance, and dividend yields becomes increasingly complex. 

Proper ETF portfolio tracking is not just about knowing your total account balance; it's about understanding your exposure, managing risk, and making informed rebalancing decisions.

## The Challenges of Manual Tracking

Many investors start by checking their brokerage accounts or using simple spreadsheets. While this works initially, it quickly falls apart due to:
- **Multiple Brokerages**: If you have a 401(k), an IRA, and a taxable account across different platforms, getting a holistic view is difficult.
- **Overlapping Holdings**: If you own an S&P 500 ETF and a Technology Sector ETF, your exposure to Apple and Microsoft is compounded. Simple trackers won't show this overlap.
- **Dividend Tracking**: Projecting future income and calculating True Time-Weighted Return (TWR) requires complex math that basic spreadsheets struggle with.

## Key Metrics Every Investor Must Track

To manage your ETFs effectively, your tracking system must provide visibility into:

### 1. Asset Allocation vs. Target Allocation
You should have a target allocation (e.g., 60% Equities, 40% Bonds). Your tracker needs to show your current allocation side-by-side with your target to highlight drift.

### 2. Geographic and Sector Exposure
Are you overly concentrated in US Tech? A good tracker breaks down your aggregate holdings by country and sector, helping you identify concentration risk.

### 3. Expense Ratios (MER)
High fees eat into long-term returns. Tracking the weighted average expense ratio of your portfolio ensures you aren't overpaying for management.

### 4. Yield on Cost & Dividend Growth
For income investors, knowing your yield on cost and how fast your dividend payouts are growing is more important than day-to-day price fluctuations.

## How to Build a Tracking System

### The Spreadsheet Approach
You can build a robust tracker using Google Sheets and the `GOOGLEFINANCE` function. This allows you to pull real-time prices and calculate current values. However, you will need to manually update share counts and handle corporate actions (like stock splits) yourself.

### Automated Portfolio Trackers
Using dedicated software connects directly to your brokerages via APIs, automatically pulling in trades, dividends, and current prices. This eliminates manual data entry and provides advanced analytics out of the box.
