Two remarks this week — one from Amazon's devices chief, one from Meta's model launch — describe the same structural change from opposite ends of the stack. Together they suggest the app is losing its place as the default unit of consumer software, and with it, some assumptions that have shaped startup strategy for fifteen years.

## The app was a distribution contract, not just software

For most of the smartphone era, shipping an app was the standard route to a consumer business because the app store bundled discovery, payments, and updates into one place. That bundle was the product as much as anything in the code.

Cal AI's trajectory illustrates how much has changed. The company generated over $30 million in revenue in two years and was acquired by MyFitnessPal in March, and by TechCrunch's account, a small army of paid influencers did much of the work. Growth came from rented attention rather than store placement. That matters because rented attention is not an asset a buyer inherits automatically; it is a rate card that can reset. If the discovery layer for a category is influencers rather than an app store, then the durable thing a company owns is its brand and retention curve, not its shelf position.

## Local models and ambient devices move the mediation point

Panos Panay, Amazon's devices chief, described a future in which ambient AI devices operate in the background, anticipating needs rather than waiting for commands. In that model the user never selects an app; intent goes directly to a system that decides what to run.

Meta's Muse Spark Open, launched with Apple and designed to run locally on Apple silicon, points at the complementary half of the shift. When a capable model runs on the device, the silicon vendor and the operating system become the mediators between user and capability. Local inference has obvious selling points — latency, offline function, and a narrower data path — but it also relocates a recurring revenue and data moat that cloud inference currently supplies. Whoever controls the device controls the default, and defaults have historically been where platform power is exercised.

## When models can act, organizational and financial norms loosen

Replit CEO Amjad Masad's "self-driving company" is an organizational claim before it is a technical one: agents handle day-to-day tasks while people make higher-level decisions. The practical consequence is that the org chart becomes an orchestration problem, and headcount stops being a proxy for capacity.

Something similar is happening in venture capital. Benchmark's acknowledgment that the long-standing norm against backing rivals is getting harder to keep reflects a real change: when AI companies converge on the same compute, data, and distribution inputs, categories blur, and informal pacts that substituted for contracts become expensive to police. Rules like these worked when boundaries were legible.

## Demonstrations are doing more work, and that is the risk

Tesla's SpaceX-powered Roadster demo in Texas, where thrusters lifted the car over a Cybercab, Cybertruck, Semi, Model Y, and Model 3, is a stunt rather than a shipping product. Intero Biosystems won Startup Battlefield at Disrupt 2026, beating 199 other companies for $100,000. Panay, meanwhile, argued publicly that even catastrophic product failures can be recovered from.

Each of these is a claim on a future category made before the category can be evaluated normally. Spectacle and narrative are rational responses when technical differentiation is hard to read from outside, but they also shift weight from evidence to presentation — a real risk when capital allocation depends on distinguishing the two.

The common thread is ownership: of the interface, the model, the distribution channel, and the narrative. Apps were never the whole business; they were the place where several kinds of control happened to intersect. As that intersection moves — toward devices, toward local inference, toward paid attention, toward agents acting on their own — the companies that fare best will likely be the ones that can say precisely what they control and what they merely rent.
