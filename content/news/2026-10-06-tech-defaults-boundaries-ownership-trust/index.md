This week’s tech news looks like a scatter plot: LibreOffice refuses AI, Bluesky promises personal domains, the Emmys move to Prime Video, Mistral releases a 1T model, Pinterest turns beauty Pins into salon plans, and Uber spends $2.3B on catering. The connective tissue is not AI alone. It is the battle over defaults—what is included, who owns identity, where distribution lives, and who executes the next step.

## No AI as a Feature, Not a Retreat

LibreOffice’s decision to keep AI out of its default configuration is easy to read as anti-hype. It is better understood as product strategy. As AI is bundled into Microsoft 365, Google Workspace, and countless SaaS tools, “no AI” becomes a boundary users can choose. The maker cites privacy, but the move also creates a distinct market position: organizations that cannot send documents to third-party models, users wary of hidden costs, and people who simply do not want generative text in their editor. The absence is the feature. This matters because defaults are policy. A checkbox buried in settings is not the same as an off-by-default architecture. If AI is opt-in, data flows and liability change. Expect more vendors to treat “no AI by default” as a procurement answer, not a philosophical stance.

## Identity and Distribution Are Being Renegotiated

Bluesky’s plan to give users their own domain on the open web is ambitious, with an 18-to-24-month timeline. The promise is portable identity: a handle that is not owned by a single platform and can move with the user. That is a direct challenge to the lock-in model of social networks. Meanwhile, the Emmys moving from broadcast TV to Prime Video in 2027—with non-Prime subscribers able to watch on the platform—shows distribution defaults shifting toward streaming bundles. These two stories point in opposite directions. Bluesky wants to reduce switching costs; Prime Video wants to become the default place for a major cultural event. Both show that “where you are” and “who you are” online are now strategic layers. Portability is hard to deliver, but it matters because platform-owned identity and exclusive distribution both raise the cost of leaving.

## AI’s Real Product Is the Plan and the Fulfillment

Mistral’s new 1T multimodal model is a capability milestone, and the race between open and closed labs continues. But the more instructive announcements are Wajo and Pinterest. Wajo, backed by Vinod Khosla, is betting that trust—not raw model quality—will win the agent market, and its Fo agent can hire humans to finish a task. Pinterest’s AI turns beauty Pins into action plans, translating looks into salon terminology, estimated costs, appointment times, and maintenance needs. These are not chatbots. They are workflow engines that convert intent into steps, and sometimes into labor. Uber’s $2.3B catering acquisition fits the same pattern: expanding from delivery into group ordering and event logistics. The model is only the engine. The product is reliable execution across payments, scheduling, vendors, and human fallback. That is why trust and liability will matter more than benchmark charts. It also means the next AI battles will be fought in operations, not just in chat windows.

## The Default Is the Product

The common thread is that tech companies are competing over defaults because interfaces are saturated. LibreOffice defaults to no AI. Bluesky wants to default to user-owned identity. Prime Video wants to default to streaming for the Emmys. Pinterest defaults to an action plan. Uber Eats wants to default to catering for group orders. Defaults determine data flows, lock-in, and who captures value. They are also hard to see, which makes them powerful. The risk is that boundary promises become marketing unless they are backed by technical portability, enforceable settings, and clear liability. The opportunity is that users and enterprises are starting to ask better questions: Is AI on by default? Can I take my identity with me? Who is responsible when an agent hires someone? Those questions will shape the next phase of competition more than any single model release.
