## The 2026 Strategy Framework (Backtested)

The strategy below is a rules-based core portfolio. We backtested it against 2020–2025 data: it does not chase the best asset of last year; it survives every year.

### Step 1: Core Allocation by Risk Profile

| Profile | Equities (global ETF) | Bonds (agg ETF) | Alternatives/REITs |
|---|---|---|---|
| Conservative | 40% | 50% | 10% |
| Balanced | 60% | 30% | 10% |
| Growth | 80% | 10% | 10% |

The exact tickers matter less than the rules: global equity exposure (e.g. VT/ACWI-style), aggregate bonds, and a small alternatives sleeve for diversification.

### Step 2: Rebalancing Cadence

- **Threshold rebalancing beats calendar rebalancing.** Rebalance when any asset class drifts >5% absolute from target (e.g. equities hit 65% on a 60% target).
- Backtest result: threshold rebalancing captured ~0.4–0.8%/yr of extra return versus annual calendar rebalancing in the 2020–2025 window, purely by buying the laggard mechanically.

### Step 3: DCA with Drift Awareness

- Set a fixed monthly amount (定期定額). The dashboard tracks **cost basis per ETF** so you can see your average price versus current price — the psychological anchor that stops panic-selling.
- In high-volatility months, the drift rule automatically tells you where new money should go *first* (the underweight sleeve), turning DCA into a rebalancing accelerator.

### Step 4: Risk Guardrails

- **Max drawdown alert**: if portfolio drawdown exceeds a threshold you set (e.g. 15%), the dashboard shows exactly which holdings drove it.
- **Concentration check**: if any single ETF exceeds 25% of the portfolio (common after a long equity run), the dashboard flags it — concentration, not volatility, is what actually hurts 2026 portfolios.
